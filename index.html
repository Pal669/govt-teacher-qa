What is EWS? (And the full forms of the abbreviations used so far.)2026-10-02

EWS = Economically Weaker Section. It is a 10% reservation for people who are NOT covered by SC, ST or OBC reservation (i.e. general category) and whose family income is below Rs 8 lakh a year, with asset limits. It was introduced by the 103rd Constitutional Amendment (2019).

Does it apply to Ashish? No. He is OBC-NCL, and EWS is only for people outside SC/ST/OBC. His reserved seats are the OBC ones.

What do I need to become a Delhi Government PGT (Economics) teacher?2026-10-01

You need two things in hand by the closing date of a DSSSB ad (eligibility is checked on that date, not on exam day):

  • Qualification: Master's in Economics / Applied / Business Economics with 50%+ aggregate, AND a B.Ed (or B.A.B.Ed / 3-year B.Ed-M.Ed) from an NCTE-recognised institution.
  • Age: not over 30. OBC (Delhi) gets +3 years (33), SC/ST +5.

Under the current rules there is no option to join first and finish the B.Ed later.

Pay: Level 8, Rs 47,600 – 1,51,100, Group B. Exam: one tier, 300 marks, 0.25 negative marking.

Is my OBC certificate valid for DSSSB?2026-10-01

Not as it is, but it's fixable.

  • It's issued by a Delhi Deputy Commissioner's office, but in the central format ("for appointment to posts under the Government of India"). DSSSB needs an OBC (Delhi) certificate.
  • The Delhi High Court ruled in July 2025 (GNCTD v. Nisha) that a certificate meant for central govt posts can't be used for Delhi govt posts.
  • Your community is on Delhi's own OBC list, so you qualify. Apply for an OBC (Delhi) certificate at your SDM office / Delhi e-District.
  • The non-creamy layer (NCL) part must be recent and issued before the ad's closing date.
  • Keep the central certificate: it's the right one for KVS and EMRS (with a fresh central NCL).

Without a valid OBC (Delhi) certificate, DSSSB treats you as General, with an age limit of 30.

What does 'first division' mean, and does it matter for me?2026-10-01

First division = 60% or more aggregate in that exam (second: roughly 45-59%, third: roughly 33-44%; exact cut-offs vary by board). If you got a CGPA, use your university's conversion formula.

Under the old Delhi PGT rules (used until early 2024), a candidate with first division in Class 12, graduation AND PG could join without a B.Ed and finish it within 3 years of joining.

That clause is gone from the current rules. It would matter again only if Delhi restores the old rules, or for NDMC posts if NDMC still uses its old rules (to verify).

What are the different ways I can become a government PGT Economics teacher?2026-09-29

Your Master's in Economics covers the subject. What every route still needs is a B.Ed from an NCTE-recognised college (simple: a teaching degree approved by India's teacher-education regulator). CTET is not needed for PGT; it only covers classes 1-8.

The recruitment routes:

  • DSSSB PGT Economics: Delhi govt schools (Directorate of Education, plus NDMC). Delhi-only posting. Best fit for staying in Delhi.
  • KVS PGT: Kendriya Vidyalayas (central govt). Transferable anywhere in India. Recent drives run jointly with NVS through CBSE.
  • NVS PGT: Navodaya Vidyalayas, residential, mostly rural campuses. Hardest on lifestyle.
  • EMRS PGT: Eklavya Model Residential Schools (NESTS, Ministry of Tribal Affairs), tribal areas across India.
  • State recruitment (e.g. Uttarakhand UKPSC, Himachal HPRCA): state schools, posting within that state. See Other Routes.

Pay for PGT is Level 8 everywhere: basic Rs 47,600 rising to Rs 1,51,100.

B.Ed options: regular 2-year B.Ed (entrance tests like DU B.Ed, IPU CET). A 1-year B.Ed for postgraduates was announced by NCTE but is not confirmed as started. IGNOU distance B.Ed needs 2 years of teaching experience first, so it does not work for you now.

Rough timeline without a B.Ed: 1-2 years for B.Ed, then the next notification, then a 6-12 month selection cycle, so about 2.5-3.5 years to actually get posted.

Correction (checked 2026-10-01): the "36" age limit for DSSSB is NOT notified. Every official DSSSB PGT ad through Oct 2025 says not over 30 (OBC Delhi 33). See the Age Limits section.

What is the meaning of Shift 1 and Shift 2?2026-10-01

A shift is a time slot on exam day. When too many candidates apply for one sitting, DSSSB runs the exam more than once that day:

  • Shift 1: morning, usually around 9 am-12 noon.
  • Shift 2: afternoon, usually around 2-5 pm.

You get one shift, printed on your admit card; you don't choose it. Each shift has a different question paper (same syllabus, pattern and difficulty), so morning candidates can't leak questions.

Normalisation (score adjustment): with several shifts, DSSSB adjusts marks so someone with a slightly harder paper isn't disadvantaged.

For practice, any shift's paper is equally useful. Male and Female PGT Economics are separate posts, so their exams are sometimes on different days.

What are NDMC schools?2026-10-04

NDMC = New Delhi Municipal Council, the civic body for the Lutyens' Delhi / Connaught Place / Chanakyapuri area (about 43 sq km). It is separate from the MCD (Municipal Corporation of Delhi) and from the Delhi Govt Directorate of Education (DoE).

  • NDMC runs its own small set of schools (Navyug Schools, Atal Adarsh Vidyalayas and other NDMC schools) in that area, including senior secondary schools that need PGTs.
  • Permanent NDMC PGTs are recruited through DSSSB, in the same combined ads as DoE PGTs, but under NDMC's own recruitment rules, so age and qualification can differ from DoE.
  • Why it matters for you: in DSSSB Advt 07/2023, NDMC PGT Economics (5 posts) allowed age 'below 36' and let first-division candidates (Class 12 + degree + PG) join without a B.Ed and finish it within 3 years. You meet the first-division condition.
  • In Advt 10/2024 NDMC sent no requisition, so its current rules are unknown. Posts are few (about 5 per cycle) and irregular.
  • NDMC also hires contract / guest teachers directly (paid per day). That is a separate, non-permanent route.
  • DU Assistant Professor comparison: no link. NDMC is a school employer; DU's gate is NET/PhD, no B.Ed, no age limit. NDMC matters only for the PGT track, as a possible no-B.Ed, higher-age back door.

Why does DA keep increasing as a percentage?2026-10-02

DA is not a raise. It only protects your basic pay from inflation.

  • Basic pay is fixed in the prices of the pay commission's base date (8th CPC: 1 Jan 2026).
  • DA (dearness allowance) = the % rise in the 12-month average of CPI-IW (consumer prices for industrial workers) since that base date. It's cumulative, so as long as prices rise, it only climbs.
  • Revised twice a year (1 Jan, 1 Jul), usually announced about 3 months late with arrears.
  • At the next pay commission, the accumulated DA is merged into basic (most of the 'fitment factor'), a real raise goes on top, and DA resets to 0%.
  • Under the 7th CPC: 0% in Jan 2016 to 60% in Jan 2026 (about 4.8% a year). Covid froze it Jan 2020-Jun 2021.
  • Knock-on effects: HRA steps up when DA crosses 25% and 50%; TA, NPS and gratuity all rise with DA.

Your real raises come from the 3% yearly increment, MACP/promotion, and the real part of each pay commission.

During PGT probation, is there a target? What do they check, and what do I need to do to clear it?2026-10-02

There is no written numeric target (like a fixed pass %) for clearing probation. The Appointing Authority judges whether you are suitable for the job, using these checks:

  • Induction training: at least 2 weeks. It is mandatory, and probation can't be completed without it (DoPT rule; also printed in recent DSSSB ads).
  • Probation report + APAR: your Principal/supervisor writes a separate probation report on top of the yearly APAR (Annual Performance Appraisal Report; simple: your yearly report card). Per DoPT, it covers your work, "outlook, character and aptitude".

  • Clean record: no vigilance case, no departmental enquiry, no penalty.
  • Results (in practice, not a written probation rule): board results in your subject matter. Delhi DoE has served show-cause notices to schools/teachers for poor results.

Any institution on a 5-day week = only 6 weeks vacation (more teaching weeks to reach 180 days). If called in during vacation, 1/3 of the days worked is credited as earned leave (cl. 14.2).

Public holidays: about 2 weeks a year (UGC cl. 14.1). DU publishes its own list, in line with central government holidays in Delhi (ESTIMATE: 14-17 days).

Leave (UGC 2018 cl. 8, same at every central university): Casual leave 8 days/year. Special casual leave up to 10 days/year (for exam duty at another university/PSC, inspections). Duty leave up to 30 days/year on full pay (conferences, invited lectures, committees). Earned leave: none in a college that gives the full 10 weeks, except 1/3 credit for vacation days worked; university departments earn it, cap 300 days, max 60 at a time; encashment as per central government rules. Half-pay leave 20 days per completed year (medical, private or academic reasons). Commuted leave up to 240 days in career (full pay, medical). Extraordinary leave without pay (max 5 years in career). Leave not due up to 360 days in career.

Family leave: maternity 180 days (twice in career); paternity 15 days (up to 2 children); child care leave 730 days in career (women); adoption and surrogacy leave as per central government rules.

Academic leave (the real edge over PGT): study leave after 3 years of continuous service as Assistant Professor, up to 3 years at a stretch, max 5 years in career, paid, counts for pension (e.g. to do a PhD). Sabbatical up to 1 year at a time (2 in career), but only after 7 years as Associate Professor or Professor. Also a Rs 3 lakh research start-up grant in social sciences (cl. 9).

Rough count of days off at a DU college (ESTIMATE): 5-day college: about 104 weekend days + ~15 holidays + ~50 vacation weekdays + 8 casual leave = about 175 days off, ~190 working days. 6-day college: about 52 Sundays + ~15 + ~60 + 8 = about 135 off, ~230 working days. Standard structure: Title, Abstract (150-250 words), Introduction (question + why it matters + your finding), Literature Review, Data & Method, Results, Discussion / Policy implications, Conclusion, References.

Working in Other States (Pay, Language, Rules)

Once I clear NET and apply to colleges in different states, what changes from state to state: HRA, other pay, rules? Do I have to learn the local language?2026-10-05

Rule 1: what changes depends on who employs you. At a central university, the central rules apply everywhere; only the city changes your HRA and transport allowance. At a state university or state government college, that state's rules decide almost everything.

HRA (House Rent Allowance), central rules: depends on the city class, not the state. X city 30% of basic, Y 20%, Z 10% (rates since DA crossed 50% in Jan 2024). At Level 10 (Rs 57,700 basic), that is Rs 17,310 in Delhi (X), Rs 11,540 in Dehradun (Y), and Rs 5,770 in Shimla or Dharamshala (Z). Rent is also much lower in Z towns, so the gap matters less than it looks.

Other central allowances: DA (Dearness Allowance) is the same everywhere. Transport allowance at Level 9 and above: Rs 7,200 + DA in the bigger cities, Rs 3,600 + DA elsewhere.

At state universities and colleges, these vary by state: (1) DA rate, which often lags the central rate; (2) the HRA slabs and city list; (3) when the state adopted the UGC 7th pay scales; (4) pension: Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Rajasthan, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh told the Centre they were going back to the Old Pension Scheme (OPS); (5) retirement age (60 to 65); (6) upper age limit in the state PSC (Public Service Commission) exam; (7) reserved seats, usually for state domiciles only, so you compete as UR (Unreserved); (8) state government college posts can be transferred across the state.

Language: central universities have no state-language rule. Exceptions: Maulana Azad National Urdu University (teaches in Urdu) and the Hindi university at Wardha.

Language: state posts often do. Tamil Nadu requires passing a Class 10 level Tamil exam for state service. Punjab requires Punjabi. Karnataka uses Kannada. Maharashtra mandates Marathi in official work (2024 Marathi Language Policy). Jharkhand requires regional or tribal languages. Himachal (HPPSC) lists 'knowledge of customs, manners and dialects of Himachal Pradesh' as desirable, not mandatory. Hindi states (UP, Uttarakhand, HP, MP, Rajasthan, Bihar, Haryana) are no problem for you.

What this means for you: central universities are the cleanest option: same rules everywhere, no language test, and your OBC certificate counts. In the Hindi belt, state universities are fine too. In the South, Maharashtra, Bengal, Odisha and the North-East, state posts usually need the local language, so stick to central universities there.

PGT comparison: the same logic applies. KVS and EMRS (central) pay HRA by city class, with no language test. DSSSB pays X-city HRA (Delhi) and has no language test. Other states' PGT jobs carry the same state-language and domicile hurdles.
