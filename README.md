## Eligibility & Documents

### What do I need to become a Delhi Government PGT (Economics) teacher?
*Asked 2026-10-01*

You need two things in hand by the **closing date** of a DSSSB ad (eligibility is checked on that date, not on exam day):

- **Qualification:** Master's in Economics / Applied / Business Economics with 50%+ aggregate, AND a B.Ed (or B.A.B.Ed / 3-year B.Ed-M.Ed) from an NCTE-recognised institution.
- **Age:** not over 30. OBC (Delhi) gets +3 years (33), SC/ST +5.
Under the current rules there is no option to join first and finish the B.Ed later.

Pay: Level 8, Rs 47,600 – 1,51,100, Group B. Exam: one tier, 300 marks, 0.25 negative marking.

### Is my OBC certificate valid for DSSSB?
*Asked 2026-10-01*

**Not as it is, but it's fixable.**

- It's issued by a Delhi Deputy Commissioner's office, but in the **central format** ("for appointment to posts under the Government of India"). DSSSB needs an **OBC (Delhi)** certificate.
- The Delhi High Court ruled in July 2025 (GNCTD v. Nisha) that a certificate meant for central govt posts can't be used for Delhi govt posts.
- Your community is on Delhi's own OBC list, so you qualify. Apply for an OBC (Delhi) certificate at your SDM office / Delhi e-District.
- The non-creamy layer (NCL) part must be recent and issued before the ad's closing date.
- Keep the central certificate: it's the right one for KVS and EMRS (with a fresh central NCL).
Without a valid OBC (Delhi) certificate, DSSSB treats you as General, with an age limit of 30.

### What does 'first division' mean, and does it matter for me?
*Asked 2026-10-01*

**First division = 60% or more aggregate** in that exam (second: roughly 45-59%, third: roughly 33-44%; exact cut-offs vary by board). If you got a CGPA, use your university's conversion formula.

Under the **old** Delhi PGT rules (used until early 2024), a candidate with first division in Class 12, graduation AND PG could join **without a B.Ed** and finish it within 3 years of joining.

That clause is gone from the current rules. It would matter again only if Delhi restores the old rules, or for NDMC posts if NDMC still uses its old rules (to verify).

### What are the different ways I can become a government PGT Economics teacher?
*Asked 2026-09-29*

Your Master's in Economics covers the subject. What every route still needs is a **B.Ed** from an NCTE-recognised college (simple: a teaching degree approved by India's teacher-education regulator). CTET is not needed for PGT; it only covers classes 1-8.

The recruitment routes:

- **DSSSB PGT Economics:** Delhi govt schools (Directorate of Education, plus NDMC). Delhi-only posting. Best fit for staying in Delhi.
- **KVS PGT:** Kendriya Vidyalayas (central govt). Transferable anywhere in India. Recent drives run jointly with NVS through CBSE.
- **NVS PGT:** Navodaya Vidyalayas, residential, mostly rural campuses. Hardest on lifestyle.
- **EMRS PGT:** Eklavya Model Residential Schools (NESTS, Ministry of Tribal Affairs), tribal areas across India.
- **State recruitment** (e.g. Uttarakhand UKPSC, Himachal HPRCA): state schools, posting within that state. See Other Routes.
Pay for PGT is Level 8 everywhere: basic Rs 47,600 rising to Rs 1,51,100.

B.Ed options: regular 2-year B.Ed (entrance tests like DU B.Ed, IPU CET). A 1-year B.Ed for postgraduates was announced by NCTE but is not confirmed as started. IGNOU distance B.Ed needs 2 years of teaching experience first, so it does not work for you now.

Rough timeline without a B.Ed: 1-2 years for B.Ed, then the next notification, then a 6-12 month selection cycle, so about **2.5-3.5 years** to actually get posted.

**Correction (checked 2026-10-01):** the "36" age limit for DSSSB is NOT notified. Every official DSSSB PGT ad through Oct 2025 says not over 30 (OBC Delhi 33). See the Age Limits section.

### What has the PGT age limit been since 2005? Was 36 the old norm, and are people fighting the change?
*Asked 2026-10-01*

**Yes: 36 was the long-standing rule, it was cut to 30 in 2022, and candidates are still pushing back.**

- **1975 - 2012:** 36 (per news sources; no DSSSB ads online from this period).
- **2014, 2017, 2020:** official ads say 36. The 2014 ad includes PGT Economics.
- **Jul 2022:** most PGTs still 36; English and Computer Science already 30.
- **29 Sep 2022:** new Delhi DoE PGT rules cut it to **30**. Protests followed in Oct 2022, and a CAT case was filed in 2023. No reversal.
- **2023 - Feb 2024:** a mix of 36 and 30, depending on when each post was requisitioned. The last 36 was PGT Economics in Advt. 07/2023.
- **Dec 2024 onward:** 30 everywhere.
- **Mar 2026:** the minister says 36 is being 'considered'. **May 2026:** new Fine Arts rules still say 30.

### What is the meaning of Shift 1 and Shift 2?
*Asked 2026-10-01*

A shift is a time slot on exam day. When too many candidates apply for one sitting, DSSSB runs the exam more than once that day:

- **Shift 1:** morning, usually around 9 am-12 noon.
- **Shift 2:** afternoon, usually around 2-5 pm.
You get one shift, printed on your admit card; you don't choose it. **Each shift has a different question paper** (same syllabus, pattern and difficulty), so morning candidates can't leak questions.

**Normalisation (score adjustment):** with several shifts, DSSSB adjusts marks so someone with a slightly harder paper isn't disadvantaged.

For practice, any shift's paper is equally useful. Male and Female PGT Economics are separate posts, so their exams are sometimes on different days.

### DU Assistant Professor (Economics) vs guest / ad-hoc lecturer: what is the difference, what is the route to a PERMANENT post, how long does it realistically take, and what does it depend on?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**Three different things:**

- **Permanent Assistant Professor:** a regular post at Academic Level 10 (Rs 57,700 basic + DA/HRA). Job security, pension (NPS), increments, and promotions up to Professor.
- **Ad-hoc:** fills a vacant post on 4-month terms, renewed again and again. Full workload (about 16 hours/week) but no security. DU replaced most ad-hocs in its 2022-24 permanent drive (about 4,500 ad-hocs; reports said about 70% were displaced in early rounds), so few ad-hoc slots are left.
- **Guest faculty:** paid per lecture (Rs 1,500, capped at Rs 50,000/month), about 8 hours/week max. DU now allows guests only against leave vacancies (maternity, study leave, etc.).
**The route to permanent (DU colleges):** UGC NET in Economics, then apply to every college advert, then a screening score of 50+ (out of 100) gets you called, then **selection on the interview alone** (screening marks carry no weight in the interview).

**Ashish's estimated screening score (based on self-reported first divisions; check against the marksheets):** graduation 60-80% = 19 + PG 60-80% = 23 + NET = 8, total **50**. That is exactly the college floor, so NET alone gets him interviews. With JRF: 52. With a PhD: about 75+. University DEPARTMENTS need 65+, so they are effectively closed without a PhD.

**A trap:** guest/ad-hoc experience earns screening marks only if the pay was at least the UGC minimum basic + DA. Guest pay (max Rs 50,000) is below that, so guest teaching usually adds 0 points. It is still worth doing for teaching practice and contacts.

**Realistic timeline (ESTIMATE):** NET takes 6-18 months (only the top 6% of those who sit the exam qualify; many need 2-3 attempts). From NET to a permanent post: 2-5 years of applying and interviewing if it goes well. If he needs a PhD to stand out, add 4-6 years. A fair overall expectation is 3-8 years, with no guarantee.

**What it depends on:** (1) clearing NET; (2) Economics vacancies, especially the OBC-reserved roster points; (3) interview performance: subject depth, a teaching demo, a research/area of interest; (4) PhD + publications (decisive for departments and many state colleges); (5) the panel's discretion, which is opaque. The 2022-24 displacement of long-serving ad-hocs shows experience alone doesn't decide it.

### What is EMRS PGT, and does it fit me?
*Asked 2026-10-01*

**EMRS = Eklavya Model Residential Schools.** Central govt boarding schools for ST students (Classes 6-12), mostly in tribal districts. Recruiter: NESTS (Ministry of Tribal Affairs); exam run by CBSE.

- **Needs:** Master's in Economics 50%+ (Economics at graduation too) AND B.Ed 50%+. The B.Ed is required.
- **Age:** 40, +3 for OBC = 43. Same window as KVS. Uses the central OBC certificate.
- **Pay:** Level 8 plus 10% special pay on basic.
- **Posting:** anywhere in India, residential duties; requests to change station are not entertained.
- **ESSE-2025:** 155 PGT Economics posts. Tier I qualifying; merit on Tier II (Economics) only.

### Why does DA keep increasing as a percentage?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**DA is not a raise. It only protects your basic pay from inflation.**

- Basic pay is fixed in the prices of the pay commission's base date (8th CPC: 1 Jan 2026).
- DA (dearness allowance) = the % rise in the 12-month average of CPI-IW (consumer prices for industrial workers) **since that base date**. It's cumulative, so as long as prices rise, it only climbs.
- Revised twice a year (1 Jan, 1 Jul), usually announced about 3 months late with arrears.
- At the next pay commission, the accumulated DA is merged into basic (most of the 'fitment factor'), a real raise goes on top, and DA resets to 0%.
- Under the 7th CPC: 0% in Jan 2016 to 60% in Jan 2026 (about 4.8% a year). Covid froze it Jan 2020-Jun 2021.
- Knock-on effects: HRA steps up when DA crosses 25% and 50%; TA, NPS and gratuity all rise with DA.
Your real raises come from the 3% yearly increment, MACP/promotion, and the real part of each pay commission.

## Lifestyle & Posting

### If I'm posted to a KVS school outside Delhi, how will I commute? Will I rent a flat, or does the government provide accommodation?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**You'd live in that town, not commute from Delhi. Either you get a staff quarter, or you rent locally and draw HRA.**

- **Staff quarters:** KVS gives govt accommodation wherever the school has its own quarters, or where the 'sponsoring agency' (the defence, PSU or govt body the KV was opened for) provides them. KVs in cantonments, PSU townships and campuses often have them.
- **Not guaranteed:** the govt told Parliament KVS builds a small set of quarters per school and has no plan to house 100% of staff. KVS had built about 14,700 quarters by 2014.
- **Allotment:** under the KVS (Allotment of Residence) Rules, 1998, by your pay level. You pay a small licence fee and **lose your HRA** while in the quarter. If you refuse an allotted quarter, HRA can be stopped.
- **If there's no quarter:** you rent a flat or room near the school and get **HRA** of 30% / 20% / 10% of basic for X / Y / Z-class cities. At the Level 8 entry basic of Rs 47,600, that's about Rs 14,280 / 9,520 / 4,760 a month.
- **Commute:** there's no transport allowance for living far away. Normal central-govt transport allowance applies, and teachers usually live close to the school.
- **Transfer costs:** when you're transferred, central rules cover moving expenses (transfer TA and a lump-sum grant). Standard rule; to verify for KVS.
Tip: in small towns the HRA is low, so a staff quarter is often the better deal. Ask about quarters when you report.

### During PGT probation, is there a target? What do they check, and what do I need to do to clear it?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**There is no written numeric target (like a fixed pass %) for clearing probation.** The Appointing Authority judges whether you are suitable for the job, using these checks:

- **Induction training:** at least 2 weeks. It is mandatory, and probation can't be completed without it (DoPT rule; also printed in recent DSSSB ads).
- **Probation report + APAR:** your Principal/supervisor writes a separate probation report on top of the yearly APAR (Annual Performance Appraisal Report; simple: your yearly report card). Per DoPT, it covers your work, "outlook, character and aptitude".
- **Documents and verification:** certificates checked against the originals, plus the OBC/NCL certificate, medical fitness, and police verification of character and antecedents. A discrepancy found at any stage cancels the appointment. The Delhi DoE has extended probation by 6 months just to finish this verification.
- **Clean record:** no vigilance case, no departmental enquiry, no penalty.
- **Results (in practice, not a written probation rule):** board results in your subject matter. Delhi DoE has served show-cause notices to schools/teachers for poor results.
- **EMRS extra:** pass the NESTS language test for the local language. Residential duties are assessed too (also in NVS).
**Outcomes:** confirmed, OR extended (max 1 year at a time; never beyond double the normal period, so at most 4 years), OR discharged with no compensation. The decision should come within 6-8 weeks after probation ends. If no order is issued, you simply stay on probation; you are not confirmed automatically.

### Is the NET 'top 6%' the top 6% of OBC candidates, or of the overall pool?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**Neither exactly. The 6% sets the total number of seats, and those seats are then split by category. You compete only with OBC candidates for the OBC seats.**

- **Step 1, minimum marks:** at least 35% aggregate across both papers for OBC-NCL (40% for General).
- **Step 2, total seats:** total qualifiers = 6% of ALL candidates who appeared in both papers (the overall pool, all subjects and categories).
- **Step 3, split:** those seats are divided by Govt of India reservation policy (OBC 27%, UR, SC, ST, EWS, etc.), and per NTA bulletins also subject-wise. The OBC seats in Economics go to the highest-scoring OBC-NCL Economics candidates.
Example (ESTIMATE, illustrative numbers): if 10,000 sit Economics, about 600 qualify; about 27% of those (~162) are OBC seats. You need to rank within the top ~162 of the OBC Economics candidates, which is why the OBC cut-off is usually lower than the General one.

**Your certificate matters:** this is a central exam, so the CENTRAL-format OBC-NCL certificate applies (the format you hold), with a fresh NCL per the bulletin's validity rule.

### Explain the whole DU professor saga step by step: NET, JRF ('GRF'), PhD, Assistant Professor, Associate Professor, Professor. My only aim is to become a permanent professor at DU.
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**'GRF' is JRF (Junior Research Fellowship).** It's the top result band in UGC NET: a paid PhD (Rs 37,000/month for years 1-2, then Rs 42,000 as SRF for years 3-5, plus HRA) on top of Assistant Professor eligibility.

**The pieces, in plain English:**

- **UGC NET:** an eligibility test, not a job. Category 1 = JRF + Asst Prof + PhD admission; Category 2 = Asst Prof + PhD admission; Category 3 = PhD admission only (does NOT make you eligible for Asst Prof).
- **PhD:** a 3-6 year research degree (UGC PhD Regulations 2022). NOT needed to become Assistant Professor; MANDATORY for Associate Professor and above. Can be done part-time while teaching (with employer NOC).
- **Assistant Professor:** the entry rank and the only realistic door into a permanent DU job. Master's 55% (50% OBC-NCL) + NET (or PhD). Selection by interview after screening.
- **Associate Professor:** middle rank. Reached by CAS promotion (PhD + 3 yrs at Level 12), or by direct recruitment (PhD + 8 yrs teaching + 7 publications + research score 75).
- **Professor:** top rank. CAS: 3 yrs as Associate + PhD + 10 publications + research score 110. Direct: PhD + 10 publications + research score 120 (+10 yrs experience). Senior Professor (Level 15) exists only in university departments.
**The pay ladder (UGC 2018, entry pay):** Asst Prof Level 10 Rs 57,700, then Level 11 Rs 68,900 (after 4 yrs with PhD / 6 yrs without), then Level 12 Rs 79,800 (after 5 more yrs), then Associate Level 13A Rs 1,31,400 (after 3 more yrs, PhD required), then Professor Level 14 Rs 1,44,200 (after 3 more yrs). Minimum about 15 years from joining to Professor, with a PhD.

**'Permanent professor':** colloquially any permanent teacher is called 'professor'. Practically, 'permanent' means getting a regular (substantive) Assistant Professor post; after 1-year probation it is secure to age 65, and Associate/Professor then come by CAS promotion from inside.

**Screening score (DU colleges, out of 100; floor 50):** Ashish with NET = 50, with JRF = 52, with PhD + JRF = about 77. Departments need 65+, so a PhD is effectively needed there.

**Sequence for Ashish (ESTIMATE):** (1) NET Dec 2026 / Jun 2027, aim for JRF (OBC JRF age limit 35, ~Feb 2030); (2) apply to every DU college Economics advert immediately; (3) register for a PhD in parallel (JRF-funded full-time, or part-time once employed); (4) permanent post realistically 3-8 years out; (5) CAS: Associate ~12 yrs in, Professor ~15 yrs in.

**PGT comparison:** PGT ladder is PGT (Level 8) to Vice Principal (Level 10) to Principal (Level 12), mostly seniority, no PhD or publications. DU ladder goes to Level 14/15 but is research-gated from the Associate stage.

### To sit for UGC NET, do I need a Master's degree?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**Yes, for the Assistant Professor route a Master's is required.** UGC NET eligibility: Master's with 55% (50% for OBC-NCL, SC, ST, PwD). Final-year PG students can also apply, but must finish the Master's with the required marks within 2 years of the NET result.

**Exception:** since 2024, a 4-year undergraduate (FYUP / honours with research) with 75% can sit NET without a Master's, but that opens JRF / PhD admission. For Assistant Professor eligibility, the UGC 2018 rule still asks for Master's 55% + NET.

**Ashish:** PG in Economics already done and meets the 55% condition, so he is eligible to sit NET now. NET is the only missing gate for DU.

**PGT comparison:** DSSSB PGT also needs a Master's (50%+ in Economics), but no NET; its missing gate is the B.Ed. So the Master's is common to both tracks; DU adds NET, PGT adds B.Ed.

### What is the idea of ad-hoc professors? Friends say they are 'Associate Professors' but not permanent, renewed semester-wise, and told me to stay away. True or rumour?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**Half true. Temporary teaching jobs are real and insecure. But a non-permanent 'Associate Professor' does not exist in DU's UGC system.**

- **Ad-hoc (the idea):** a stop-gap teacher hired against a vacant permanent post while the college delays permanent hiring. 4-month terms, renewed again and again, full workload, no job security. In DU many served 10-15 years this way; the 2022-24 permanent drive replaced most of them (reports: ~70% displaced in early rounds).
- **Associate Professor is a senior rank:** you reach it only by (a) CAS promotion from a permanent Assistant Professor post (PhD + about 12 years), or (b) direct recruitment (PhD + 8 years + 7 publications + research score 75). Both mean a permanent post. So in a DU / government-funded college, 'Associate Professor on semester contract' is not a real category.
- **What your friends most likely are (ESTIMATE):** (1) private university/college staff where 'Associate Professor' is a designation on a contract renewed per semester/year; or (2) ad-hoc, guest or contractual Assistant Professors loosely calling themselves professors; or (3) teachers in self-financed courses on contract. Ask them: which institution, and what does the appointment letter say (regular / ad-hoc / contractual / guest)?
- **What 'stay away' should mean:** don't treat ad-hoc/guest/contract as a road to permanency. Years spent there do not convert into a permanent post; only a fresh interview for a permanent advert does. Experience adds at most 10 screening points (2/year), and only if paid at least UGC minimum basic + DA, so guest teaching adds 0.
- **When temporary work is useful:** teaching practice, interview confidence and contacts while you clear NET/PhD. Fine as a stepping stone, never as the plan.
**PGT comparison:** Delhi govt schools have guest teachers (renewed per session, no security) - the same trap. A DSSSB PGT selection is permanent from day one (after probation), just like a permanent DU Assistant Professor post.

### What percentage clear NET? If so many people clear it, so many show up for interviews, how can a panel possibly interview that many?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**About 6% of everyone who sits NET qualifies (all subjects, all categories).** That is the rule: total qualifiers = 6% of candidates who appeared in both papers.

- **Economics, per cycle:** OBC Assistant Professor qualifiers were 500-625 per cycle (Jun 2024 to Dec 2025, PW data). If OBC is ~27% of seats, all-category Economics qualifiers are roughly 1,850-2,300 per cycle, ~4,000+ a year (ESTIMATE).
- **The pool keeps growing:** NET is valid for life, so every year's qualifiers stay in the pool. Tens of thousands of people hold NET in Economics (ESTIMATE).
**How panels handle the crowd:**

- **Screening filters only weakly:** colleges call everyone with 50+ out of 100, and NET-only is exactly 50. So a college with 2-4 Economics posts can call hundreds. One report (IP College, 2023) said about 600 were shortlisted across departments for 8 posts (unverified).
- **Interviews run over several days in short slots:** one selection committee (principal, head of department, two subject experts, the VC's nominee, a reserved-category observer) sees dozens of candidates a day. That means only minutes each (exact length unverified).
- **The same people apply everywhere:** most candidates apply to every college, so the 'crowd' is the same few thousand people moving between interviews.
**What this means for Ashish:** in a short interview the panel only remembers what stands out: deep command of your area (Money & Banking, Macro, Indian Economy is a genuine edge), a clear research area, a PhD or publications, and composure. NET-only at 50 puts you in the crowd; a PhD (~77) moves you up the shortlist, and university departments need 65+.

**PGT comparison:** DSSSB PGT is decided by written-exam merit rank. It's transparent, with no interview and no panel discretion. The DU route is the opposite: the exam (NET) only gets you in the room, and the interview decides.

### What is this '50+' screening score? How is it calculated from percentages, and does clearing NET give everyone 50?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**NET does NOT give you 50. NET gives you 8 points. The 50 is the TOTAL of your graduation marks + PG marks + NET.** So people have different scores.

**DU college screening table (out of 100):**

- **Graduation:** 80%+ = 21 | 60-80% = 19 | 55-60% = 16 | 45-55% = 10
- **Post-graduation:** 80%+ = 25 | 60-80% = 23 | 55-60% (50% for OBC-NCL/SC/ST/PwD) = 20
- **M.Phil:** 60%+ = 7 | 55-60% = 5
- **PhD:** 25 (M.Phil + PhD together max 25)
- **NET with JRF:** 10 | **NET:** 8 (max 10)
- **Awards:** national/international 3 | state 2 (max 3)
- **Research publications:** 2 each in peer-reviewed / UGC-listed journals (max 6)
- **Teaching / post-doc experience:** 2 per year (max 10), only if paid at least the UGC minimum basic + DA
**Cut-off:** colleges call everyone at 50+; university departments need 65+. SC/ST/PwD get a 5-mark relaxation (not OBC). Ties go to higher Master's marks. The score is only for shortlisting and carries no weight in the interview.

**Examples:** grad 80%+ and PG 80%+ with NET = 21+25+8 = 54. Grad 55-60% and PG 55-60% with NET = 16+20+8 = 44, which is NOT called. **Ashish (60-80% in both, to confirm from marksheets) = 19+23+8 = 50, exactly the floor.**

**How Ashish can raise it:** JRF (+2, 52); 1-3 research papers (+2 each, up to 58); a PhD (+25, about 77 with JRF); paid teaching at UGC scale (+2/year).

**PGT comparison:** DSSSB has no screening score. Your written-exam marks decide everything.

### So after clearing NET I'm eligible; I apply whenever there's an advert, and if I clear the interview I get the job? No prior teaching experience needed? Ad-hoc maybe?
*Asked 2026-10-02*

**Yes, that's right, with three small corrections.**

- **Correct NET result:** only Category 1 (JRF) or Category 2 makes you eligible. Category 3 (PhD only) does not.
- **Order:** you don't wait for an interview call. You apply to each college advert online first. Screening (50+) then decides whether you get called, and the interview decides the job.
- **After selection:** appointment as permanent Assistant Professor, then 1-year probation, then confirmation.
**No prior teaching experience is required** for Assistant Professor (UGC 2018 lists only Master's 55%/50% OBC-NCL + NET or PhD). Experience only adds screening points (2/year, max 10, and only at UGC pay).

**Ad-hoc is optional, not a step you must take.** Useful for teaching practice and interview confidence; it never converts into a permanent post by itself.

**Practical points:** adverts come college by college at irregular times, so you must keep watching DU's recruitment page and college websites. DU is a central university, so OBC posts use the central OBC-NCL certificate (the format you hold, with a fresh NCL).

**PGT comparison:** PGT is the same idea but with an exam instead of an interview: B.Ed + Master's, apply to the DSSSB ad, and the written-exam rank decides the job. No experience needed there either.
